How Therapy Works

Every patient is uniquely different -- that's why treatment is planned individually.

The Four Goals of Therapy

These aren't separate habits -- they're one system, repeating thousands of times a day and night.

1

Nasal Breathing

Filters and humidifies every breath for optimal health.

2

Tongue Resting Position

Tongue resting on the roof of the mouth shapes the palate from the inside.

3

Lips Closed at Rest

Sealed lips make nasal breathing possible and support facial development.

4

Correct Swallowing

Proper eating, chewing, and swallowing reinforces all three other goals.

Because these patterns repeat so constantly, they influence facial growth, orthodontic stability, airway health, eating and feeding, and speech coordination. When one part is off, the others compensate -- and that compensation causes issues over time.

What to Expect

1

Assessment

A full evaluation to understand your unique needs and build your treatment plan.

2

Exercise Program

You'll be prescribed targeted myofunctional exercises to practice at home between visits.

3

Progress Reviews

Appointments every 3-4 weeks to evaluate progress, adjust exercises, and advance through the program.

4

Habituation

As movements become easy or mastered, new targets are selected until new patterns are automatic.

5

Graduation & Follow-Up

Once you graduate, re-check visits over 3-12 months ensure lasting retention.

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to some of the questions we hear most often.

Many factors can interfere with normal orofacial muscle function, including long-term pacifier or thumb/finger sucking habits, nail biting, lip licking, bottle feeding, a restricted airway (from enlarged tonsils or adenoids, a deviated septum, or chronic allergies), chronic mouth breathing, tongue, lip, or buccal ties, and hereditary factors.

Orofacial Myofunctional Disorders (OMD) can play a large role in craniofacial development and can have long-term effects. They can relate to physical appearance and the shape of the mouth, orthodontic relapse, feeding difficulties, jaw joint pain, dental bite and alignment, head and neck posture, sleep patterns, airway development, and speech patterns.

The correct tongue resting position is fully on the roof of the mouth. Tongue position matters because the tongue is a major facilitator for orofacial growth and development. When it rests on the roof of the mouth, it helps stimulate the bone of the maxilla to grow in a more ideal way.

When the tongue is not on the roof of the mouth, we can start to see a collapse of the dental arch, crowding of the teeth, and a non-ideal bite. Over time this can affect appearance, create a high vault in the palate (which can affect nasal airway volume), and contribute to orthodontic relapse, posture problems, and airway issues.

Tongue-tie is a complex issue that can affect many people unknowingly, and its impact can be very different from person to person. The tongue performs many complex functions such as elevating, retracting, protruding, and lateralizing. When a small piece of tissue holds the tongue down, a patient may experience difficulty with eating, chewing, posture, swallowing, and speech, and even stomach upset from swallowing extra air.

When you see a Certified Orofacial Myologist, we complete a functional assessment to see whether there is a dysfunction of the orofacial muscles and whether a tongue-tie is contributing. If a release is needed, we work with patients before and after the procedure to optimize the outcome.

Many speech sound errors are rooted in oral dysfunction. Speech production relies on precise coordination of oral structures and muscle function. Before or alongside speech-specific work, addressing underlying oral dysfunction, including resting posture, swallowing patterns, and muscle tone, removes barriers to progress and improves outcomes.

Our integrated model examines oral rest posture, oral function, and structural readiness, then trains new patterns of movement. If specific speech sound work is required, it is incorporated once improved movement patterns have been learned.
